About the App

What is Aumazing?

🧩

Structured Learning Support at Home

Aumazing gives parents a structured way to guide short learning activities across communication, social, play, and attention. It may assist home practice, but it is not a diagnostic or treatment tool, does not replace professional care, and cannot guarantee changes in a child's development.

12 Learning Activities
4 Learning Domains
🛡️
Target scope and responsible use

Following guidance from a SPED teacher, the primary user-testing scope is children ages 2–6 who have received an ASD Level 1 or Level 2 diagnosis from a qualified professional. This is the project's target group, not a strict age restriction: diagnosed children with ASD outside ages 2–6 may still use the app when its activities are appropriate for them and an adult provides supervision. Children without ASD may also use the learning activities, although Aumazing was specifically designed to support children with ASD. Aumazing only reports educational skill bands for activity selection; it does not assign an ASD diagnosis, severity level, or treatment plan.

How the Scoring Works

These rules are verified against Aumazing v1.1.0-run3. They explain the deterministic educational rubric that converts recorded gameplay into skill bands and a priority-based module recommendation. They are not medical criteria and cannot diagnose ASD.

🔎
What “published rules” means

Percentages below are the release defaults and offline fallback values. Authorized administrators can configure accuracy, completion, prompt-dependency, turn-taking, and idle-time cutoffs, so an actively configured installation may use different values. Retry, interruption, and random-touch limits shown here are fixed in this release.

🧮

How the Inputs Are Calculated

  • Accuracy: average of score ÷ total items across relevant sessions.
  • Completion: average recorded completion rate; accuracy is used when completion is unavailable.
  • Prompt dependency: average recorded dependency score; prompts ÷ total items is the fallback.
  • Turn-taking: average recorded success rate; accuracy is the fallback.
  • Sessions with zero total items are excluded. With no valid sessions—or after an unexpected scoring error—the safe result is Emerging or Variable Attention.
🎵

Sensory Preference Rule

performance = 60% accuracy + 40% response speed

Response time is normalized against 5 seconds. Each music, haptic, or combined condition is compared with the baseline: a change of at least +10% is treated as helpful, while −10% or lower is treated as a reason to avoid that condition. If no baseline exists, the result is “No Sensory Support Needed.”

🧭

How My Path Chooses a Module

The first matching rule wins, in this exact order:

  1. 1
    All four areas are at their highest bandAdvanced Practice Module
  2. 2
    Three or more areas need supportMixed Support Module
  3. 3
    Communication needs supportCommunication Starter Module
  4. 4
    Social interaction needs supportTurn-taking Starter Module
  5. 5
    Play skills need supportPlay Skills Starter Module
  6. 6
    Attention needs supportAttention Support Module
  7. 7
    No earlier rule matchesFoundation Skills Module

User Testing Guide

Help Us Test Aumazing

Please complete one full learning cycle with your child. Your experience helps us evaluate whether Aumazing is usable and whether it can assist parents with structured home practice.

👨‍👩‍👧
Step 1

Enter Child Information

Create the child profile and configure sensory preferences. A parent or guardian should supervise the session.

🎯
Step 2

Pre-Assessment

Let the child complete the pre-assessment. The result is an educational skill profile, not a diagnosis.

🧭
Step 3

Play My Path

Open My Path or choose a module recommended from the pre-assessment, then let the child complete the activity.

✅
Step 4

Post-Assessment

After the recommended learning activity, complete the post-assessment to close the testing cycle.

📥
Step 5

Download Both Results

If applicable, tap Download in the upper-right of each Assessment Result screen and save the pre- and post-assessment files.

📝
Step 6

Complete the Survey

Complete our post-test Google Form and attach the requested results only when applicable and with your consent.

Open Post-Test Survey ↗
Why we need your help

Your feedback and the pre/post result files serve as capstone testing artifacts. They help us examine usability and whether the app appears to assist parents during structured practice. They do not establish clinical effectiveness or prove developmental change.

Protect your child's privacy

Submit only what the survey requests. Remove or cover names and other identifying details, and share assessment files only if you consent to their use as anonymized capstone evidence.

Primary Target: Ages 2–6

Children ages 2–6 with a professional ASD Level 1 or Level 2 diagnosis who use the activities under adult supervision.

Children with ASD outside ages 2–6 and children without ASD may also use the app, while recognizing that it was designed primarily for children with ASD.
